# Resume Parsing Benchmark — Results

**Models:** Qianfan-OCR, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, Docling (full), Docling (simple), Qwen3.5-4B, Claude Haiku (via OpenRouter)
**Datasets:** `resume_dataset_hf` (real English resumes), `synthetic_cv` (100 Arabic + 100 English, generated)
**N:** 200 docs per dataset per model

---

## Metric definitions

- **CER / WER** — Character / Word Error Rate. % of characters or words wrong vs. ground truth. Lower is better. WER is stricter: one wrong letter makes the whole word count as wrong.
- **micro** — all documents pooled into one ratio (weighted by length).
- **mean** — average of each document's own score (every document counts equally).
- **median** — the middle score (ignores outliers — tells you the typical document, not the average).
- **CI95 (lo/hi)** — 95% confidence range for the metric.
- **skills-F1** — fuzzy-matched precision/recall/F1 on extracted skills (>=90% string similarity counts as a match).
- **education / experience accuracy** — field accuracy on matched records (jobs/degrees matched to their closest ground-truth counterpart first, then scored).
- **lang_ar / lang_en** — same metrics, split by language, for `synthetic_cv`.
- **Docling (full/simple)** — deterministic PDF text-layer extraction, no GPU, no VLM.
- **Claude Haiku** — API-based (OpenRouter), not a local model. Real per-call cost, unlike the other five.

**Ground truth note (`resume_dataset_hf`):** originally used the source dataset's own NER `SKILL` tags, which were confirmed noisy (contact-info fragments and generic nouns mistagged as skills) and had no education/experience labels at all. Ground truth was regenerated by running the same GPT-4.1 extraction used to score every pipeline's predictions, but against the clean, reliable source text instead of any pipeline's OCR output. This fixed skills-F1 and added education/experience scoring for the first time. Caveat: since predictions are also GPT-4.1-extracted, this measures "did OCR preserve enough fidelity for GPT to extract the same fields," not agreement with independent human judgment — still a real signal (a low-CER pipeline's predictions should closely match this ground truth, a high-CER one shouldn't), just not human-verified.

## Headlines

1. **Claude Haiku leads on Arabic, on `synthetic_cv`'s overall average, and now on `resume_dataset_hf`'s field extraction** — the strongest model across the widest range of conditions once field metrics became trustworthy.
2. **Margins between the top 3 (Qianfan, Qwen3-VL, Claude Haiku) are often small** — several differences (e.g. skills-F1 0.629 vs 0.633 vs 0.634 vs 0.651) are close enough that confidence intervals likely overlap. Treat "the best model" as a range, not a single winner, when the gap is a few points.
3. **Qianfan remains the most consistent (best median) model** on `resume_dataset_hf` and Arabic CER/WER, even where it loses the average — lowest variance, not necessarily highest peak accuracy.
4. **Fixing `resume_dataset_hf`'s ground truth changed the story completely** — skills-F1 went from an unusable ~0.0005 (all six models) to a real, differentiating 0.62–0.65, and education/experience accuracy went from "not scored" to ~0.95–0.98. The original NER-tag ground truth was never usable; the GPT-extraction-from-clean-text replacement is.
5. **Qwen3.5-4B remains unreliable on Arabic** — its micro/mean CER/WER are far worse than the other five, driven by severe repetition on a subset of documents.
6. **Docling (simple mode) is the best free/local/no-GPU option** — competitive skills-F1 (0.619–0.958 across datasets) and beats Qwen3.5-4B broadly, at zero GPU cost.
7. **Claude Haiku is not universally best** — Qwen3-VL and Qianfan both edge it out on clean English text quality, and Qwen3-VL clearly wins `synthetic_cv` experience-record accuracy (0.949 vs. 0.904).
